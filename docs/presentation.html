The Core Problem

Modern hearing aids apply the same noise-reduction algorithm to every user — regardless of their individual hearing loss profile.

Why This Is Wrong

Every person's hearing loss is different. Person A loses high frequencies. Person B loses low frequencies. The same algorithm cannot be optimal for both.

Three Different Audiograms

dB HL = decibels Hearing Level. Higher = worse hearing at that frequency.

Our Solution

A neural network that reads each person's audiogram and personalises its noise-reduction behaviour per user.

Key Concept

What Is an Audiogram?

An audiogram is the result of a clinical hearing test. It records the quietest sound a person can hear at 6 standard frequencies.

Why This Is Our Model's Input

The audiogram tells us exactly which frequencies the person struggles with. Our model uses this to selectively preserve the most important frequency bands for that specific person.

⟶ Hearing aids already personalise amplification per audiogram (NAL-R prescription). We personalise noise reduction — this is the gap.

Literature Review & Research Gap

What Exists — And What Doesn't

What Already Exists

  • Generic DNN (Deep Neural Network) denoising (Diehl et al., 2023, Nature) — restored speech to near-normal intelligibility. No audiogram conditioning.
  • Conv-TasNet (Luo & Mesgarani, 2019) — SOTA waveform-domain separation. Generic.
  • SEMamba (Chao et al., 2024) — current SOTA PESQ 3.69. No hearing-aid awareness.
  • Clarity Challenge (University of Salford, 2021–2024) — hearing-aid-specific benchmark. Winning systems use audiograms for scoring, not conditioning.
  • NeuroAMP (Ahmed et al., 2025, IEEE TAI) — audiogram conditioning for amplification, not noise reduction.
  • FiLM (Perez et al., 2017) — conditioning mechanism used in vision tasks.

The Gap We Target

Gap 1

No published system uses an audiogram as a conditioning input to a noise-reduction neural network (only used for amplification or post-hoc evaluation).

Gap 2

SOTA architectures (SEMamba, CMGAN) are not evaluated on hearing-aid-specific metrics (HASPI/HASQI) and ignore the <10ms latency constraint.

Our Specific Novelty

Mamba/SSM + FiLM conditioning + MetricGAN+ trained with HASPI discriminator. No paper combines all three. The HASPI-GAN specifically is unpublished.

Models 1 & 2

Classical Signal Processing Baselines

Model 1 — Classical

Wavelet DWT Denoising

Converts the audio signal into a wavelet representation — similar to a spectrogram but with adaptive time-frequency resolution. Noise appears as small coefficients everywhere; speech concentrates at specific scales.

DWT(signal) → coefficients at levels 1..L
threshold λ = σ × √(2 × log(N))
soft_threshold(coeff) → IDWT → clean signal
  • Wavelet: db8 (Daubechies-8), decomposition level 5
  • Noise σ estimated via Median Absolute Deviation (MAD)
  • Library: PyWavelets (pywt)
Model 2 — Classical

MMSE-LSA Filter

Minimum Mean Square Error – Log Spectral Amplitude. The algorithm actually running in commercial hearing aids. Uses a decision-directed approach to estimate signal-to-noise ratio smoothly over time, then applies a perceptually-motivated gain.

ξ(k,n) = α×Â²(k,n-1)/λ_n + (1-α)×max(γ-1, 0)
α = 0.98, ξ = a priori SNR, γ = instantaneous SNR
  • No machine learning — pure signal processing
  • Virtually zero latency
  • Reference: Ephraim & Malah (1985), IEEE TASLP

These are the baselines every DL model must beat. MMSE-LSA is not a toy — it's the industry standard.

Model 3

1D Convolutional Neural Network — Conv-TasNet Style

Deep Learning — No Audiogram

Works on the raw audio waveform directly — no STFT (Short-Time Fourier Transform) spectrogram needed. Learns its own internal frequency decomposition from data. Achieves very low latency.

Learned Encoder (1D Conv: kernel=16, stride=8)
Replaces STFT — N=256 learned filters
↓

↓
Mask Estimation → Sigmoid

↓
Learned Decoder → Enhanced Waveform
No audiogram input

Why Dilated Convolutions?

Standard 1D convolution with kernel=3 sees only 3 frames. To see context 1 second into the past you'd need 100+ layers.

Dilation lets the kernel skip frames:

dilation=1: sees [t-1, t, t+1]
dilation=8: sees [t-8, t, t+8]
dilation=128: sees [t-128, t, t+128]
→ receptive field: 511 frames at 16 kHz = 32ms
Role in Our Comparison

Model 3 answers: "What does a state-of-the-art generic DNN achieve without any personalisation?"

Models 4 and 5 must beat this using the audiogram. The difference = benefit of personalisation.

Implementation: Asteroid library, Conv-TasNet recipe. Loss: SI-SDR (Scale-Invariant Signal-to-Distortion Ratio).

Innovation 1: FiLM Conditioning

The audiogram is fed to a small auxiliary network that outputs scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters. Applied to bottleneck features: output = γ × features + β. Different audiograms → different processing.

Innovation 2: Complex Ratio Mask (CRM)

Predicts both real and imaginary mask components. Eliminates "musical noise" artifacts caused by discarding phase. Previously called IRM (Ideal Ratio Mask) only handled magnitude.

Innovation 3: MetricGAN+ with HASPI Discriminator ★

A discriminator network learns to predict HASPI scores. The generator trains adversarially to maximise HASPI — directly optimising for hearing-impaired listeners. This specific combination is unpublished.

FiLM — Feature-wise Linear Modulation

How the Model Reads the Audiogram

FiLM is a conditioning mechanism that allows a neural network to modify its own internal behaviour based on a side input (the audiogram). It applies a learned scale-and-shift operation to intermediate feature maps.

Audiogram vector a = [a₁, a₂, a₃, a₄, a₅, a₆]
6 hearing thresholds, normalised 0→1
↓
FiLM Generator (small MLP: 6→64→128→512)
Learns what hearing loss means for features
↓
Scale γ
(256 values)
Shift β
(256 values)
↓
output = γ × bottleneck_features + β

Reference: Perez et al., "FiLM: Visual Reasoning with a General Conditioning Layer", AAAI 2018

What FiLM Does in Practice

For Severe High-Frequency Loss

γ and β learned to amplify the high-frequency noise-detection channels → model becomes more aggressive at removing high-frequency noise that the person can't hear anyway

For Flat Moderate Loss

Different γ and β → model adapts uniformly across all frequency channels

Verification Test

Critical test: run the same noisy clip with two different audiograms. The output masks MUST be numerically different. If they're identical, FiLM is not being used — we investigate gradients.

assert max(|mask_normal – mask_hf_loss|) > 0.01
✅ Personalisation verified
Model 5 — State-of-the-Art

Mamba / State Space Model (SSM) + FiLM

Current SOTA — SEMamba PESQ: 3.69

Why Mamba Beats Transformers for Audio

PropertyTransformerMamba
ComplexityO(T²)O(T)
Real-time modeNeeds maskingNaturally causal
Best PESQ~3.403.69
Parameters10–30M2–5M
Standard SSM: h(t) = A×h(t-1) + B×x(t)  [A,B fixed]
Mamba (selective): A(t),B(t) = f(x(t))  [input-dependent]
→ Gates memory based on what is currently heard
Why This Is Critical for Hearing Aids
  • Linear cost: doesn't slow down as audio gets longer
  • Naturally causal: no future frames needed → real-time streaming
  • Higher accuracy: PESQ 3.69 vs ~3.40 for Transformers
  • Smaller model: fits in constrained hardware budgets
Simple Analogy

A Transformer reads a book by comparing every word to every other word simultaneously. Mamba reads left-to-right, keeping smart notes in a selective notebook — remembering speech onsets, forgetting steady noise.

Our Addition: FiLM Conditioning

SEMamba has no audiogram input. We add a FiLM layer after the Mamba blocks. This combination is not published. Reference: Chao et al., 2024 (SEMamba).

STFT — Short-Time Fourier Transform

Converts raw audio samples into a 2D complex spectrogram — the "photo of sound". F=257 frequency bins, T=frames. Required before applying the mask.

Complex Ratio Mask (CRM)

Unlike the basic IRM (Ideal Ratio Mask), the CRM modifies both magnitude AND phase. Results in significantly fewer musical-noise artifacts in the output.

FiLM — Key Bridge

This is where the audiogram enters the model. The FiLM layer generates personalised scale and shift parameters that change the model's processing for each user.

Dynamic Mixing Strategy (Training)

Instead of creating fixed noisy files, we mix clean speech + noise on-the-fly every training epoch:

noisy = clean + α × noise
SNR drawn uniformly from [-5, +10] dB
noise file: random from MUSAN/ESC-50
→ effective infinite training data
Audiogram Strategy

Clarity Challenge includes real listener audiograms for training. For TIMIT-based training, we sample synthetic audiograms from a realistic distribution of hearing loss profiles based on WHO/clinical statistics.

Why NOIZEUS for Evaluation

NOIZEUS is the standard evaluation corpus in the SE (Speech Enhancement) literature. Every classical algorithm paper uses it — our results are directly comparable to the published literature.

Evaluation

Metrics — Why Standard Metrics Aren't Enough

Hearing-Aid Specific Metrics

HASPI — Hearing Aid Speech Perception Index

Predicts how well a hearing-impaired listener would understand the speech. Simulates the impaired cochlea. Takes audiogram as input. Range: 0→1 (higher = better intelligibility).

Reference: Kates & Arehart (2021). Implementation: pyclarity

HASQI — Hearing Aid Speech Quality Index

Same architecture as HASPI, but predicts perceived quality (naturalness, absence of distortion) rather than intelligibility. Also takes audiogram. Range: 0→1.

Primary Expected Finding

Models 4 and 5 show a larger HASPI improvement over Model 3 than STOI improvement. This proves audiogram conditioning specifically benefits hearing-impaired listeners — not just audio quality in general.

Latency Constraint

Mamba (Model 5) is expected to meet the <10ms hearing aid latency requirement in causal mode. U-Net (Model 4) may need knowledge distillation to a smaller student model to reliably meet it.

Audiogram-Specific Finding

HASPI improvement largest for severe HF loss audiogram. Smallest for near-normal hearing. This validates the personalisation hypothesis.

Novelty & Contribution

What Makes This Project Original

1
Audiogram-Conditioned Noise Reduction

NeuroAMP (2025) uses audiograms for amplification. No published system uses audiogram conditioning for the noise reduction step. We are the first to apply FiLM to noise reduction specifically.

2
HASPI-Optimised GAN Training ★ Strongest Claim

MetricGAN+ trains a discriminator on PESQ. We train the discriminator on HASPI — which takes the audiogram as input and measures intelligibility for hearing-impaired listeners. Not published anywhere.

3
Mamba/SSM + FiLM for Hearing Aids

SEMamba (2024) achieves SOTA PESQ 3.69 but has no audiogram conditioning. We add FiLM to SEMamba-style architecture for personalised HA enhancement. The combination is novel.

4
Systematic 5-Model Comparison with HA Metrics

A rigorous comparison from classical DSP through SOTA DL, evaluated on HASPI/HASQI across multiple audiogram profiles, has not been published in this form. Directly useful to the field.

Future Scope

Where This Goes Next

🔬
Real Hardware Testing

Deploy the Mamba+FiLM model on actual hearing aid DSP (Digital Signal Processor) chips — e.g. Arm Cortex-M55 + Ethos-U55 NPU (Neural Processing Unit). Benchmark true on-device latency, power draw, and memory footprint. Validate that the <10ms constraint holds in silicon, not just simulation.

📄
Research Paper Submission

The HASPI-optimised MetricGAN+ discriminator + audiogram FiLM conditioning is an original contribution. Target venues: ICASSP 2027 (IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing), Interspeech 2027, or IEEE/ACM TASLP (Transactions on Audio, Speech, and Language Processing). Deadline for ICASSP 2027 is ~Sep 2026.

🌐
Indian Language Testing

Evaluate robustness on Indian languages — Hindi, Gujarati, Bengali, Tamil. Use IndicSpeech or MUCS (Multilingual and Code-switching ASR) datasets. Indian speech has different phoneme inventories and retroflex consonants — a model trained only on English may fail. This is a genuinely underexplored gap in the HA literature.

🔐
Federated Learning

Audiograms are private medical data. Federated Learning (FL) trains the model across many hearing aid users without sending raw audio or audiograms to a server — each device trains locally and shares only gradient updates. Privacy-preserving personalisation at scale — combines with FiLM perfectly.

📱
Companion Mobile App

A smartphone app where hearing-impaired users input or import their audiogram (from a photo of their audiogram chart). The app connects to the hearing aid via Bluetooth LE, uploads the audiogram vector, and the model re-conditions itself in real time. Audiogram update = instant personalisation with no clinic visit.

🏥
Clinical User Study

Recruit 20–30 hearing-impaired participants, play enhanced audio via calibrated headphones, collect subjective intelligibility and quality ratings. Compare our system vs their current hearing aids. This is the gold standard validation — aligns with the Clarity Challenge's listener evaluation methodology.

The Highest-Impact Next Step

Submit a paper to ICASSP 2027 on the HASPI-optimised MetricGAN+ finding. This is the specific result that has not been published — and it is directly testable from the deliverables of this minor project.
